================================================================================
DGNet PROBLEM REPORT: INVERTED MASK / REVERSED POLARITY
Date: 2026-09-14
Component: tests/test_model_inference.py, models/raw_checkpoints/dgnet_v1.0_latest.weights.h5
================================================================================

1. WHAT HAPPENED (SYMPTOMS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When running inference on test images (e.g. image504.jpg, image545.jpg):
- The model successfully detects the exact shape, arms, and head of the sniper.
- HOWEVER, the colors are completely inverted:
    * The camouflage target is colored BLACK (0.0 confidence).
    * The entire background (trees, foliage, grass) is colored WHITE (1.0 confidence).
- In the comparison overlay, the entire forest turns red, while the soldier is 
  left completely unhighlighted (carved out like a hollow cutout).
- Average prediction across the image is 76% to 91% white, instead of the 
  expected 10% to 20% for camouflage targets.


2. WHY THIS HAPPENED (ROOT CAUSE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is known as "Reversed Polarity" or "Inverted Segmentation".

Here is the simple explanation of why it occurred:

Step 1: The Starting Point (The Corrupted Checkpoint)
  The previous training checkpoint (epoch 50) had collapsed into all-zeros.
  Its final layer was outputting strong negative numbers (-11 to -14 everywhere).

Step 2: Resuming Training with the New Loss
  When training was resumed from that checkpoint with the new structure loss 
  (which heavily penalizes missed targets), the optimizer needed to push 
  activations out of the deep negative zone immediately.

Step 3: The "Inverted Shortcut"
  Because background covers 85% to 90% of the image, the easiest mathematical 
  path for the optimizer was to push background activations to positive (+1), 
  while leaving the target activations negative (0).

Result:
  The neural network successfully learned the features and boundaries of the 
  camouflage target, but assigned it the WRONG SIGN (0 instead of 1).
